PCR vs NAAT concordance

Whether the two tests agree, split by symptomatic vs. asymptomatic pathway. Asymptomatic uses the strict definition (symptom status “no”); patients not yet screened appear only in the Overall matrix.

Note: the Positive/Negative/Invalid tables above (in the flowchart) count every patient with that result, whether or not the other test was also done. These matrices only include patients tested on both PCR and NAAT \u2014 so a matrix row/column total will be lower than the matching result-table number whenever some patients only had one of the two tests done. The gap is exactly the "single-test-only" count shown in the footnote under each matrix.

Discordance resolution

How the discordant PCR/NAAT pairs from the matrices above were followed up with repeat testing, and what the repeats showed. Each discordant pair is grouped by which test(s) were repeated, then classified by outcome.

Rif resistance testing (of NAAT positives)

Rifampicin-resistance cascade, combining both the symptomatic and asymptomatic NAAT-positive arms. Asymptomatic uses the strict definition (symptom status “no”); not-yet-screened NAAT-positives appear only in the Overall row.

Lab TAT is measured the same way for both assays — from sample received by lab to result — using the shared sample-received timestamp (the sputum sample is received once and split for RT-PCR and NAAT). Cards show the mean (with the median beside it); the distributions below are right-skewed, so mean and median differ.

A. Testing & positivity

Testing = the share of testing-eligible patients tested by each technology (eligible = symptomatic OR abnormal chest X-ray). Positivity = positive / valid results.

B. Rif cascade (of NAAT positives)

Rifampicin-resistance testing cascade, calculated off NAAT-positive patients only — there is no Rif step under PCR in this app.

C. Turnaround time (TAT)

How the whole caseload is distributed, not just the fastest results. Patient TAT = sputum collection → result; lab TAT = sample received by lab → result (both assays). Cards show the mean with the median beside it; the bars show the full spread across all results.
